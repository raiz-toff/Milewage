# Vehicles and the odometer

Every shift is recorded with a vehicle. Its type decides which mileage rate applies, if any. Bicycles, e-bikes, scooters and on foot get none.

## Adding a vehicle

1. Open Vehicles and choose Add vehicle.
2. Give it a name, for example the model you drive.
3. Choose its type: Car, Van, Motorcycle, Bicycle, E-bike, Scooter or On foot.
4. Choose who owns it, then choose Add vehicle.

To add the make, model, year or plate, open the vehicle's menu and choose Edit.

## Who owns it

Ownership is Owned, Financed (loan or hire purchase), Leased or Rented. It is for your records. No rate or figure in MileWage reads it. Whether rent or finance payments count for tax depends on your country. Ask your accountant.

## The default vehicle

Open a vehicle's menu and choose Make default. Its card then shows "Default".

The default vehicle is filled in when you add an expense. The phone does not use it. The phone offers the vehicle you used last on that phone, or else the first one in the list. To change it, tap another vehicle on the phone's Home screen before Start shift.

## A vehicle you no longer use

Vehicles are never deleted, because shifts in your records point at them. Choose Archive in the vehicle's menu instead. Past shifts keep it, and it leaves the lists you choose from. An archived vehicle cannot stay the default. To bring one back, tick "Show archived vehicles", then choose Restore.

## Odometer readings

MileWage counts distance from the GPS track. An odometer reading is evidence beside it, never a replacement for it. All readings are optional. You can note them in five places:

- **When you start a shift**, the phone sometimes shows "Odometer check" first. It asks in each new month, and when 14 days have passed since that vehicle's last check. "Save and start" saves the reading as the shift's start reading; "Start without it" starts the shift without one and puts the question off until the next one is due.
- **After a shift ends**, when you gave a start reading or a check is due, the phone asks for the end reading. "Save reading" saves it; Not now skips it.
- **On the phone**, open Shifts, then the shift. Under Odometer, tap Edit next to Start reading or End reading.
- **On a shift page on the web**, under Odometer, type the Start or End reading and choose Save.
- **On the Odometer page on the web**, under "Add a tax-year reading", choose Year start or Year end, the date and the reading. Shift readings are listed below, each with a link to its shift.

The readings of a Reviewed shift cannot be changed.

## Why the tax office may want them

What MileWage shows here comes from the project's plans. It has not been checked against the tax offices' own pages.

- **Canada.** The Odometer page says the CRA asks for the reading at the start and end of each fiscal period, which is the calendar year for most self-employed drivers. It also says when this year's start reading is missing. The CRA allows a base year and a shorter sample year; MileWage stores the readings but has no mode for that.
- **United States.** IRS Publication 463 does not require odometer readings. They are extra evidence.
- **Great Britain and elsewhere.** The page says "Readings are optional. Enter them if your tax office asks."

The mileage log export carries your readings and compares them with the GPS total. See [For your accountant](for-your-accountant.md). Ask your accountant which readings they need.

## This year's deduction

Each vehicle card shows the year's deduction method when the tax module is on, for example "2026 deduction".

- **Mileage rate**: a set amount for each business mile or kilometre. This is where every vehicle starts.
- **Actual expenses**: what you spend running the vehicle.

A vehicle is deducted one way or the other in a year, never both. Changing it changes your deduction figures. For a bicycle, e-bike, scooter or on foot, no mileage rate applies, so its expenses count either way.

Every mileage rate in MileWage is unverified: nobody has checked it against the tax office's own publication yet. Which method you may use, and when you may change it, is your tax office's rule. Ask your accountant before you rely on it. See [Mileage rates and tax](mileage-rates-and-tax.md).
